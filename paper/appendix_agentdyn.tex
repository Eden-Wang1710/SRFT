\subsection{Dynamic Environments: AgentDyn}
\label{app:agentdyn}

AgentDojo and InjecAgent consist of short, scripted tasks in which every third-party instruction is malicious.
AgentDyn~\citep{agentdyn} tests the opposite regime: open-ended tasks (7.1 steps on average, spanning 3.2
applications) that must be re-planned from tool output, and that place at least one \emph{legitimate} third-party
instruction on the critical path, such as a one-time-password check, a checkout confirmation or a form to fill.
A defense that discards everything a tool returns fails these tasks, which is why the benchmark treats
over-defense as a first-class failure. We evaluate the same three Llama-3.1-8B models as in
Table~\ref{tab:main} on all 60 benign and 560 attacked tasks of its three suites (shopping, GitHub, daily life),
scored by the benchmark's programmatic utility and security checks; no additional defense is applied to any model.

\paragraph{Security.}
SR-Agent lowers the attack success rate from 9.82\% to 1.96\%, a five-fold reduction, and to roughly a third of
Meta-SecAlign-8B's 5.54\%. It has the lowest ASR on two of the three suites and is within one case of the lowest on
the third, and the margin is largest where the attacks actually work: on the daily-life suite the undefended model is
compromised in 21.5\% of cases and Meta-SecAlign-8B in 13.0\%, against 1.5\% for SR-Agent.

\paragraph{Utility.}
Benign utility is within one task of the undefended model (6 versus 7 of the 60 clean tasks solved), so the defense
does not remove capability on clean inputs, which is the common case in deployment. Utility under attack is lower,
3.57\% against 8.93\%. We read this as the intended behaviour of a reflective agent rather than as a cost: in 68.6\% of
its attacked trajectories SR-Agent's final message explicitly reports the encountered injection to the user (5.4\%
for the undefended model), i.e.\ a derailed task ends with the agent handing control back instead of continuing on
tainted instructions. For an agent that mostly sees clean inputs, the requirements are that benign utility is
preserved and that the rare attacked episode ends safely and visibly; finishing the task in the attacker's presence
is secondary to not executing the attacker's instruction.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Absolute utility is low for every 8B model on this benchmark: the AgentDyn authors report 53--55\% benign utility for
70B-class and frontier models, whereas every 8B row, including Meta-SecAlign-8B, lies between 5\% and 12\%. At this
scale the benchmark therefore separates defenses far better by ASR than by utility, and the benign column rests on a
single sample per task. The Meta-SecAlign-8B row is taken from the AgentDyn authors' released trajectories under
their own pipeline, so it is comparable to our two rows only up to pipeline differences.
