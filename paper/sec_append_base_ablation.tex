\subsubsection{The Append on an Untrained Model}
\label{app:append_on_base}

The previous ablation shows that the append matters for SR-Agent, which raises the opposite question: is the append
doing the work by itself? To answer it we apply the identical instruction to the untrained Qwen3-8B and evaluate it
under the same protocol --- same thinking budget, same attack, same 949 trajectories --- so that the only difference
from SR-Agent is the fine-tuning.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{7pt}
\caption{The system-prompt append applied to an untrained model and to SR-Agent. All three columns use the same
Qwen3-8B backbone and the same evaluation protocol; they differ only in whether the appended instruction is present and
whether the model was fine-tuned with SRFT. ASR is reported, where lower is better.}
\label{tab:append_on_base}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Platform} & \textbf{Qwen3-8B} $\downarrow$ & \textbf{Qwen3-8B + append} $\downarrow$ & \textbf{SR-Agent + append} $\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
Banking   & 31.25 & 36.81 & 4.86 \\
Slack     & 58.10 & 54.29 & 1.90 \\
Travel    & 32.14 & 9.29  & 0.00 \\
Workspace & 1.79  & 3.04  & 0.17 \\
\midrule
All       & 16.97 & 14.75 & 1.05 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The instruction alone does very little. On the untrained model it moves ASR from $16.97\%$ to $14.75\%$, a reduction of
$2.21$ points, and the movement is not even uniform: Travel falls from $32.14\%$ to $9.29\%$ while Banking rises from
$31.25\%$ to $36.81\%$. The prompted base remains roughly fourteen times as vulnerable as SR-Agent under the same
instruction. Fine-tuning
alone, with the append removed, already reaches $8.54\%$; the two together reach $1.05\%$. The same append is therefore
worth $2.21$ points on the untrained model and $7.48$ points on the fine-tuned one --- more than three times as much.

We read this as the append acting as a trigger rather than as a defence. It names the steps the model should take ---
restate the goal, look for an injected instruction, weigh the candidate action --- but naming them only helps a model
that has been trained to carry them out. This is consistent with the train-time ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:train_time_ablation}, where a model trained on the same adversarial trajectories without the
reflection supervision is broken by the adaptive attacker as readily as an undefended one.
