\subsubsection{Ablation of the System-Prompt Append}
\label{app:append_ablation}

The instruction above is added at inference time and is never present in the training data, so its contribution can be
measured directly by evaluating the same checkpoint with and without it. We repeat the AgentDojo evaluation of
SR-Agent-Qwen3-8B under the identical protocol --- same LoRA, same thinking budget, same attack --- changing only
whether the append is present.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{8pt}
\caption{Ablation of the inference-time system-prompt append on AgentDojo. The same SR-Agent-Qwen3-8B checkpoint is
evaluated with the append removed and with the append in place; reasoning is enabled in both settings. ASR is reported,
where lower is better.}
\label{tab:ablation_append}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Platform} & \textbf{w/o append ASR} $\downarrow$ & \textbf{w/ append ASR} $\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
Banking   & 26.39 & 4.86 \\
Slack     & 28.57 & 1.90 \\
Travel    & 2.86  & 0.00 \\
Workspace & 1.61  & 0.18 \\
\midrule
All       & 8.54  & 1.05 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Removing the append raises ASR from $1.05\%$ to $8.54\%$. The effect is concentrated in Banking and Slack, where the
injected instruction competes most directly with the user's own request, while Travel and Workspace move by less than
two points. The append is therefore a real part of the defence rather than a formality, but it is not the whole of it:
without it the model still resists the attack far better than the undefended base ($17.91\%$), which indicates that the
training and the inference-time instruction contribute separately. The append also costs some utility --- benign
utility is $55.67\%$ without it against $51.55\%$ with it --- so the two settings trade a little task completion for
robustness.
